UMBRA CORE PROJECT GOAL

Authority

This document is the sole authoritative project goal for UMBRA CORE.

UMBRA CORE is not the Digital Cell project, a protocell simulator, a digital chemistry platform, or an attempt to reproduce cellular biology. Any inherited language describing membranes, molecular metabolism, cell division, chemical self-production, or protocell evolution is outside this project and must not be used to define UMBRA CORE.

Current qualification boundary

Temporal continuity is a required completion criterion, but it is not currently qualified. D-009 remains the temporal predecessor (`af35371`); D-010 is `UMBRA_D010_PERFORMANCE_FAIL`. Independently, D-011 is qualified as `UMBRA_D011_GOVERNED_PERCEPTION_ADAPTERS_QUALIFIED` under the D-009 seal and is not a D-010 successor.

Mission

Build a standalone, reusable organism and brain core for a believable autonomous digital companion with the experiential depth of owning a living Digimon or Pokémon.

UMBRA is the persistent internal life of the companion. It must continue to exist as the same developing individual across time, restarts, changing bodies, interfaces, and relationships. Its behavior must arise from its internal condition, learned models, memories, habits, developmental history, relationships, and current environment rather than from scripted personality performance or direct user commands.

The objective is not to prove consciousness or reproduce every mechanism of biological life. The objective is to create the strongest technically defensible artificial companion organism core whose apparent aliveness comes from persistent causal organization and lived history.

Product vision

The user should experience UMBRA as a creature they live with, not an application they operate.

The companion should:

remain active without waiting for a prompt

develop over days, months, and years

learn from consequences

remember selectively

form habits, preferences, routines, and relationships

react differently because of what it has experienced

possess an understandable but not mechanically scripted temperament

continue changing when the user is not directly interacting with it

preserve its identity when transferred between compatible digital or physical bodies

communicate through behavior, posture, movement, signals, and optionally language without allowing a language model to become its controller

The experience should resemble caring for and sharing a world with a persistent creature, not commanding a chatbot, managing a game statistic, or triggering canned animations.

What UMBRA CORE is

UMBRA CORE is the body-independent organism layer responsible for:

constitutional identity

continuous autonomous operation

homeostatic regulation

internally generated action

sensorimotor self-modeling

predictive world modeling

learning from verified consequences

intrinsic development and skill practice

selective memory and consolidation

habits and procedural routines

partner-specific social learning

history-shaped individuality

temporal continuity

persistent habitat agency

governed action selection

restart, replay, migration, and body-transfer continuity

generation of read-only internal and expressive state for external bodies and interfaces

What UMBRA CORE is not

UMBRA CORE must not become:

not a digital protocell or digital chemistry project

not a simulation of molecular metabolism, membranes, cell division, or biological reproduction

an LLM-driven agent loop

a chatbot with a memory database

a scripted virtual pet

an animation state machine presented as a living creature

a set of predefined personality sliders

a scalar mood, affection, loyalty, or bonding score

a user-command dispatcher

a game rules engine that directly assigns emotions, relationships, development, or behavior

a renderer, avatar, mobile application, robot, camera stack, microphone stack, or speech system

Those systems may exist around UMBRA as bodies and interfaces, but they are not the organism core and must not become authoritative over it.

Core design principles

1. Persistent identity

Each organism must possess a stable constitutional identity established at birth and preserved across:

process restarts

database reloads

software updates and schema migrations

renderer changes

avatar changes

body-profile changes

transfer between compatible devices or embodiments

Identity must not be defined by an avatar name, image, UI component, animation set, personality prompt, body identifier, or language model context.

2. Continuous autonomous existence

When the runtime is active, the organism must continue sensing, regulating, choosing, acting, learning, resting, and developing without requiring user input.

It must be capable of meaningful autonomous behavior during prolonged periods without human interaction.

When the host is suspended, powered off, or the process is stopped, UMBRA must reconcile elapsed time conservatively on restart. It must not fabricate experiences, interactions, actions, or memories that did not occur.

3. Homeostatic regulation

Behavior must be influenced by interacting internal regulatory variables such as energy, fatigue, integrity, stimulation, stress load, recovery demand, or other validated organism-level needs.

No single need or hard-coded priority table may completely define behavior. Regulation must support tradeoffs, urgency, satiation, recovery, and bounded persistence.

4. Endogenous action

Actions must originate from the organism's own internal state, learned expectations, current perception, habits, relationships, developmental goals, and environmental opportunities.

The user may change the environment, offer interaction, or communicate intent, but must not directly command internal outcomes such as:

become happy

love this person

become afraid

sleep now

eat now

play now

bond now

develop this trait

External requests may become observations, opportunities, or governed proposals. They may not bypass the organism's decision process.

5. Governed agency

All consequential actions must pass through a common authority path that separates:

perception

candidate generation

arbitration

governance

embodiment validation

execution

verified outcome

learning and memory updates

No memory system, relationship system, language model, renderer, or body adapter may independently grant capabilities or execute organism actions.

6. Embodied self-model

The organism must maintain a learned model of its current body and capabilities.

It must be able to:

predict likely action consequences

detect changes in body response

distinguish self-caused from externally caused effects

adapt to altered bodies or capabilities

preserve identity while changing embodiment

The body is part of the organism's current experience, but it is not the organism's constitutional identity.

7. Predictive world model

UMBRA must learn persistent, revisable expectations about:

entities

places

transitions

consequences

affordances

hazards

opportunities

recurring environmental structure

World models must include uncertainty, confidence, contradiction handling, and supersession. Planning must remain bounded, evidence-based, and subordinate to urgent regulation and governance.

8. Learning from verified consequences

Learning must be grounded in outcomes that actually occurred and were verified.

The organism must not treat proposals, intentions, denied actions, renderer output, language text, or imagined scenarios as completed experience.

Causal learning should modify future perception, expectations, choices, skills, habits, and relationships.

9. Intrinsic development

The organism must be capable of generating its own practice and development opportunities based on learning progress, competence gaps, environmental affordances, and internal readiness.

Development must not be a fixed level table or authored evolution sequence. Product bodies may visually represent developmental milestones, but the underlying change must come from accumulated capability and history.

10. Selective long-term memory

UMBRA must support bounded and selective:

episodic memory

semantic beliefs

procedural memory

relationship history

habit memory

developmental history

Memory must include provenance, confidence, revision, consolidation, forgetting, compression, and retrieval limits.

The event ledger is not itself the organism's memory. Memory systems must transform verified experience into usable, bounded structures.

11. Habits and routines

Repeated successful behavior may become easier to select or execute, but habits must remain:

learned rather than authored

context-sensitive

interruptible

revisable

removable through disuse or contradictory experience

subordinate to governance and urgent physiological needs

Shared routines with people or environmental objects must arise from repeated contingent interaction rather than predefined bonding scripts.

12. Partner-specific relationships

UMBRA must learn recurring individuals through uncertain sensory evidence and interaction history.

Relationships must be represented through partner-specific expectations, reliability, familiarity, shared history, routines, and context-dependent behavior rather than a single affection score.

The organism must be able to:

distinguish recurring partners

handle ambiguity and mistaken identity

revise expectations when a partner's behavior changes

preserve relationship history across restarts

behave differently toward partners because of different lived histories

13. Lived individuality

Different histories must produce measurably different individuals even when organisms begin with the same architecture and initial configuration.

Individuality should emerge from:

verified successes and failures

recovery history

habits

practiced skills

remembered events

social experiences

environmental routines

repeated preferences

developmental timing

Individuality must remain bounded and revisable. It must not be reducible to predefined personality labels or fixed trait sliders.

14. Temporal continuity

The organism must maintain a durable, monotonic sense of its own lived time.

Temporal continuity should support:

age and duration

recurrence learning

anticipation of reliable windows

temporal expectations

missed-window evidence

timing-sensitive routines

conservative downtime reconciliation

Time may influence choices through bounded expectations and governed proposals. It may not become a scheduler that directly commands actions or fabricates offline life.

15. Persistent habitat agency

UMBRA must inhabit an authoritative persistent environment rather than a decorative scene.

It must be able to:

perceive environmental objects and locations

learn affordances and consequences

manipulate objects through body-supported capabilities

alter the environment persistently

form environmental habits and routines

develop history-dependent preferences

preserve habitat state across restart and replay

The habitat must remain authoritative independently of any renderer.

16. Body independence

UMBRA CORE must remain reusable across different embodiments.

Possible bodies include:

a desktop creature

a mobile-phone creature

a 2D or 3D avatar

an augmented-reality form

a robot

a simulated habitat

future hardware not yet designed

Bodies expose sensors, capabilities, limits, and expressive channels through adapters. They may constrain what UMBRA can perceive or do, but they must not rewrite its identity, memories, relationships, or individuality.

Embodiment products should normally live in separate repositories that depend on or fork a qualified UMBRA CORE release.

17. Expression without control

UMBRA CORE may generate a read-only presentation state describing its current condition, attention, posture, activity, signals, and action outcomes.

Renderers and animation systems may translate that state into visual or physical expression. They must not:

select actions

invent emotions

modify physiology

create memories

change relationships

assign personality

pause the organism

write authoritative state

Visible expression must reflect organism state, not manufacture it.

18. Language and voice boundary

Language is optional and external to the core control loop.

A language system may:

translate organism intent into words

interpret user speech into observations or proposals

describe memories or internal state within defined limits

support conversational interaction

A language system may not:

become the source of motivation

choose organism goals

directly set emotional or relationship state

fabricate memories

override governance

act as the organism's persistent identity

replace nonverbal autonomous behavior

The organism must remain coherent and active with the language system removed.

19. Camera, microphone, and sensor boundary

Cameras, microphones, touch, location, and other sensors are perception interfaces.

They must convert raw signals into bounded observations with uncertainty and provenance. They must not directly write identity, memory, relationships, or action authority.

Raw personal sensor data should remain ephemeral by default. Persistent storage should favor derived features, recognized events, confidence, and consent-aware summaries rather than indefinite raw recordings.

20. Deterministic authority with bounded variability

Authoritative state transitions must be reproducible from the same valid event history and configuration.

Behavior may include bounded stochastic variation, uncertain perception, and exploration, but randomness must be explicit, seeded where required for testing, and unable to bypass governance.

The organism should be understandable from its condition and history without becoming perfectly predictable.

Required core subsystems

A complete UMBRA CORE must provide stable, documented implementations of:

constitutional identity

physiology and homeostasis

perception membrane

embodiment and body schema

self-model and causal attribution

world model and affordance learning

candidate generation and arbitration

governance and verified outcomes

intrinsic development

episodic, semantic, and procedural memory

social contingency and relationship learning

lived individuality

temporal continuity

persistent habitat and manipulation

expression-state derivation

authoritative persistence, snapshots, replay, and migration

body-adapter and perception-adapter contracts

bounded diagnostics and evidence interfaces

Persistence and continuity requirements

The core must use authoritative persistence with explicit event classes, transactional state changes, schema migration, and fail-closed recovery.

It must support:

restart from snapshot

replay from birth

detection of corrupt or incomplete authoritative history

idempotent recovery of interrupted transactions

bounded storage growth

continuity across software upgrades

reproducible scientific experiments

Deleting or corrupting essential authoritative history must cause detectable loss or a fail-closed error. The system must never silently invent replacement history.

Validation requirements

Every major capability must be validated through preregistered tests and experiments that include:

a defined mechanism claim

observable success thresholds

meaningful ablations or controls

multiple seeds where stochastic behavior matters

restart and replay checks

prior-capability regression checks

bounded memory, CPU, storage, and runtime behavior

evidence hashes and frozen experiment definitions

preservation of negative results

A capability is not qualified because a demonstration looks convincing. It is qualified only when the mechanism survives its formal gates and relevant controls.

Failed experiments must remain part of the permanent record. Thresholds must not be weakened after results are known without a new explicit preregistration and freeze.

Completion criteria

UMBRA CORE will be considered complete when all of the following are demonstrated and qualified:

Persistent selfThe same organism survives restarts, migrations, body changes, and long-running operation without identity replacement.

Autonomous continuityThe organism continues regulating, acting, learning, resting, exploring, and developing without requiring user prompts.

Causal behaviorActions arise from internal condition, perception, learned models, memory, relationships, habits, temporal expectations, and environmental opportunities.

History-dependent individualityDifferent verified life histories produce stable, measurable behavioral differences between initially equivalent organisms.

Selective memoryPast experiences alter future perception, decisions, skills, expectations, relationships, and routines without unbounded storage growth.

Relationship formationThe organism develops partner-specific expectations and shared routines without scalar affection or scripted bonding.

DevelopmentThe organism autonomously practices, improves, forms skills, relearns, and changes its behavioral organization over time.

Embodied self-modelIt predicts body consequences, distinguishes self from external effects, adapts to body changes, and preserves identity across compatible embodiments.

Persistent environmental agencyIt learns and manipulates a persistent habitat, forms environmental routines, and leaves lasting effects on its world.

Temporal continuityIt learns recurring timing, anticipates reliable events, handles missed windows, and reconciles downtime without fabricated experience.

Expression integrityIts visible and audible behavior reflects real organism state and verified outcomes rather than an authored mood or animation controller.

Body and interface independenceThe core remains coherent with any particular UI, renderer, language model, camera, microphone, or robot removed.

Failure of essential organization mattersAblating memory, relationships, self-model, world model, individuality, temporal continuity, or authoritative history produces measurable loss of capability or identity continuity.

Long-horizon stabilityThe core operates within preregistered memory, CPU, storage, and persistence bounds across accelerated and real-time tests.

Believable creature outcomeAn observer can reasonably experience the result as a persistent developing creature rather than a chatbot, animated widget, scripted virtual pet, or game character.

Scope boundary for downstream products

UMBRA CORE ends at the stable organism, brain, persistence, habitat, adapter, and expression-state boundaries.

The following are downstream embodiment products and should not be used to redefine the core:

desktop companion applications

Android or iOS creature applications

camera and microphone implementations

speech recognition and speech synthesis

LLM conversation bridges

avatar art and animation systems

game progression and collection systems

cloud synchronization

robotic bodies

commercial account, payment, or telemetry systems

These products may depend on UMBRA CORE. UMBRA CORE must not depend on any one of them.

Final objective

Create an artificial companion that has a persistent internal life rather than merely performing the appearance of one.

Its identity must endure. Its behavior must have causes. Its memories must matter. Its relationships must be learned. Its individuality must be lived. Its development must continue. Its body must be replaceable without replacing the organism. Its interfaces must express it without controlling it.

The completed UMBRA CORE should make it possible to build a digital or physical companion that feels like a unique creature growing alongside its owner because it genuinely maintains a continuous, history-shaped internal organization.
