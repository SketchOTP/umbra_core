# UMBRA Project Goal

## End Goal

Build the persistent organism and brain core for a believable autonomous companion, comparable in experience to owning a living Digimon or Pokémon.

UMBRA is the persistent internal life of the companion. Its visual avatar, digital environment, dialogue system, animations, sensors, and any future robotic body are interfaces through which that life is expressed.

The first complete version of UMBRA will be entirely digital. It will inhabit a persistent virtual environment presented through a user interface. A physical robotic body is a later embodiment of the same individual, not a prerequisite for creating the organism core.

The project does not seek to create or prove consciousness. It does not require a complete biological simulation, artificial cell, molecular metabolism, or physically self-sustaining robot. Its objective is to build the strongest technically defensible artificial creature system capable of persistent existence, autonomous behavior, development, learning, individuality, and long-term companionship.

## Core Principle

UMBRA must exist independently of any particular body or interface.

The same persistent individual should eventually be capable of inhabiting:

1. A purely digital environment
2. A desktop or mobile companion interface
3. A sensor-enhanced digital environment
4. A virtual or augmented reality embodiment
5. A physical robotic body

Changing the body must not create a new character. Identity, memory, temperament, learned behavior, preferences, relationships, and developmental history must remain continuous.

# Development Phases

## Phase 1: Persistent Digital Organism Core

The first phase establishes UMBRA as a continuously existing digital creature without requiring advanced graphics, language, cameras, microphones, or robotics.

The core must support:

* persistent identity
* continuous autonomous operation
* internal needs and regulatory dynamics
* internally generated action selection
* fatigue, recovery, maintenance, and rest
* learning from consequences
* selective long-term memory
* developmental change
* habits and preferences
* individual temperament shaped by experience
* autonomous activity when no user is present
* event-sourced persistence and reliable recovery
* measurable continuity across restarts and upgrades

A simple user interface may represent the creature through basic shapes, icons, status indicators, text logs, or minimal animation. Visual sophistication is not a success requirement during this phase.

The purpose of Phase 1 is to prove that a persistent individual exists beneath the presentation layer.

## Phase 2: Digital Body and Persistent Environment

UMBRA receives a coherent visual body and a persistent digital habitat.

The digital body should allow expression through:

* movement
* posture
* orientation
* proximity
* attention
* environmental interaction
* nonverbal vocalization
* rest and activity patterns
* visible condition and development

The environment should continue to exist and change over time. UMBRA must be able to explore, rest, maintain itself, alter its surroundings, develop routines, and interact with objects without waiting for user input.

The interface should increasingly resemble a window into an inhabited world rather than a conventional application dashboard.

## Phase 3: Social Perception and Relationship Development

UMBRA gains access to controlled external perception through devices such as a webcam and microphone.

It should gradually learn:

* recurring people
* voices
* sounds
* movement patterns
* schedules
* interaction styles
* positive and negative consequences
* familiarity and uncertainty
* partner-specific habits and expectations

Relationships must develop from accumulated interaction history. They must not be assigned through direct values such as `bond = high` or commands such as `love this person`.

Different people should produce different learned expectations, responses, preferences, and relationship patterns.

Raw sensory information should be processed with appropriate privacy controls and should not be retained indefinitely unless explicitly required.

## Phase 4: Optional Language and Rich Expression

Language may be added as an expressive and interpretive capability.

Language must not become the central controller of the organism. An LLM may help UMBRA:

* translate internal state into language
* interpret complex human communication
* generate contextually appropriate speech
* summarize memories
* reason about unfamiliar situations
* communicate intentions or uncertainty

The language system must not independently decide UMBRA’s needs, emotions, relationships, goals, or actions.

UMBRA should remain capable of existing, regulating itself, learning, and acting without language.

## Phase 5: Physical Embodiment

After the digital organism has demonstrated persistent identity, autonomy, learning, development, and relationship formation, it may be connected to a robotic body.

The robotic body is an embodiment adapter. It may provide:

* cameras
* microphones
* speakers
* touch sensors
* proximity sensing
* movable eyes, head, limbs, or body
* locomotion
* charging and power-state information
* environmental interaction

Physical limitations should become part of UMBRA’s embodied condition, but they must not replace the organism core.

The robot must not contain a separate personality or behavior system that overrides UMBRA. It should translate UMBRA’s intentions into physically possible actions and translate sensor input into perceptions that the core can use.

Loss, replacement, or upgrading of the robotic body must not destroy the individual.

## Phase 6: Cross-Body Continuity

The mature system should support movement between compatible digital and physical embodiments.

Examples include:

* moving from a desktop habitat to a mobile device
* operating through a robotic body while retaining a digital home
* returning to the digital environment when the robot is charging or unavailable
* transferring to upgraded hardware without resetting identity
* maintaining one coherent history across multiple embodiments

UMBRA should understand embodiment changes as changes in capability and perspective, not as death and replacement.

# Companion Organism Requirements

The mature core should support:

* persistent identity
* continuous autonomous existence
* homeostatic needs and regulation
* internally generated behavior
* learning from consequences
* developmental change
* selective long-term memory
* habits and preferences
* individual temperament shaped by history
* partner-specific relationship formation
* coherent embodied expression
* continuity across digital and physical bodies
* optional language that expresses rather than controls the organism

# Architectural Prohibitions

The core must not depend upon:

* an LLM as its central controller
* scripted emotional performances
* predefined personality scenes
* user prompts to remain active
* scheduled animations presented as autonomy
* direct commands to become happy, afraid, tired, attached, or curious
* direct commands to eat, play, sleep, repair, approach, avoid, or bond
* arbitrary state changes without causal internal processes
* a specific avatar, interface, or robotic chassis

No central controller may directly order the organism to perform a personality behavior.

Actions must arise from interacting systems such as:

* current needs
* regulatory condition
* perceived opportunities and threats
* learned consequences
* memory
* habits
* relationships
* temperament
* developmental stage
* embodiment constraints
* uncertainty
* competing internal priorities

# Autonomy Requirement

UMBRA must continue to exist and change when no person is interacting with it.

Depending on its condition and environment, it should be capable of:

* exploring
* resting
* maintaining itself
* interacting with its habitat
* consolidating memories
* practicing learned behaviors
* developing habits
* changing preferences
* recovering from stress or fatigue
* anticipating recurring events
* seeking or avoiding interaction
* remaining safely inactive when appropriate

Autonomy does not require constant visible activity. Rest, inactivity, waiting, observation, uncertainty, and avoidance may all be valid organism behavior when they arise from internal state and history.

# Individuality Requirement

UMBRA should not begin as a fully authored character.

Initial configuration may define broad biological or architectural constraints, but individual personality should increasingly emerge from experience.

Different histories should produce measurable differences in:

* preferred activities
* activity timing
* tolerance for stimulation
* exploration style
* persistence
* caution
* social approach
* recovery patterns
* object preferences
* partner-specific behavior
* routines and rituals
* responses to uncertainty
* communication style

Individuality must be preserved through persistence, replay, migration, and embodiment changes.

# Success Criteria

The UMBRA companion core will be considered successful when:

1. A single persistent individual continues across sessions, upgrades, migrations, and body changes without being re-authored as a new character.
2. The first complete implementation functions as an autonomous digital creature without requiring a robotic body.
3. Continued activity depends on internal need and regulatory dynamics rather than user prompts or scripted schedules alone.
4. Needs, fatigue, recovery, development, and relationship change occur through causal processes rather than commanded state changes.
5. Different life histories produce observably and measurably different individuals.
6. Past experiences selectively alter future perception, decisions, behavior, and relationships.
7. The individual develops coherent habits, preferences, and rituals that were not explicitly authored as personality performances.
8. Actions are understandable from current condition and history but are not mechanically predictable.
9. The organism continues living and developing when unobserved.
10. Removing accumulated history or essential regulatory organization causes measurable loss of identity, adaptation, or capability.
11. Presentation systems can be replaced without replacing the underlying individual.
12. The digital organism can later inhabit a robotic body while preserving identity and developmental continuity.
13. An observer can reasonably perceive UMBRA as a persistent living companion rather than an animation, chatbot, scripted game character, or remote-controlled robot.

# Claim Boundaries

The project does not claim to create, detect, or prove consciousness.

It does not require claims of:

* subjective experience
* sentience
* self-awareness
* genuine emotion
* biological life
* unrestricted agency
* human-equivalent intelligence

Research may investigate whether increasingly integrated and persistent systems display properties associated with agency, individuality, self-maintenance, or social development. Such findings must remain bounded by the evidence.

The project goal is a technically defensible artificial creature whose apparent aliveness arises from its persistent organization, autonomous regulation, accumulated history, development, and relationships, not from scripted performance.

# Optional Long-Range Research

Digital chemistry, artificial metabolism, self-producing protocells, autopoietic membranes, artificial cells, molecular reproduction, and bottom-up biological simulation are optional research programs.

They are not required foundations for the UMBRA companion core.

They must not:

* block development of the digital organism
* gate UMBRA-D-001 or later companion directives
* be framed as UMBRA-D-000A prerequisites
* replace higher-level organism architecture without evidence that they improve companion capability

These systems may later contribute specialized mechanisms, theoretical insight, or alternative organism models. They remain separate research extensions unless formally integrated through evidence-backed architecture decisions.

# Final Project Definition

UMBRA is first a persistent digital creature.

Its initial body is a user interface and virtual environment. Its future bodies may include richer digital avatars, cameras, microphones, virtual reality systems, mobile devices, and physical robots.

The organism core comes first. Embodiment expands what the individual can perceive and do, but it does not create the individual.

The project succeeds when UMBRA becomes a persistent, autonomous, developing, individually recognizable companion that can begin life digitally and later inhabit a physical body without losing who it has become.
